# South Atlantic gray triggerfish, SEDAR 82 (2024)

## Unit and assessment status

NOAA Stock SMART row 10063 identifies the South Atlantic gray triggerfish
(*Balistes capriscus*) unit. It is separate from the Gulf of Mexico unit
represented by Stock SMART 10062. SEDAR 82 is the latest detailed assessment
located for this unit: a 2024 Research Track assessment using the Beaufort
Assessment Model, with data through 2021. The model period is 1982-2021.

The research-track assessment was peer reviewed, and the panel supported its
methods and data decisions. The reviewer described it as suitable to carry
forward to an operational assessment. NOAA's peer-review listing has no
actual dissemination date, and NOAA's species page still says that a formal
South Atlantic assessment has not been conducted. The reviewed sources do not
establish that SEDAR 82 became the current operational or management-adopted
production assessment. This identity is therefore screened as new, but its
eligibility for the current production-assessment set remains unresolved.

## Inputs and outputs

The BAM assessment uses commercial and recreational landings and discards,
fishery age and length compositions, and one combined fishery-independent
abundance index from the Southeast Reef Fish Survey chevron traps and related
trap-video survey. The fishery age bins were 1-5+ for 1982-2014 and 1-8+ from
2015. The DW-05 paper provides survey abundance indices and age/length
compositions from 1990-2021; no survey sampling occurred in 2020. The survey
operates mainly from May through September. Its annual age compositions are
potentially sufficient to reconstruct an age-specific index, but that would
not be the native aggregate-index likelihood. The assessment also chose not
to fit two available fishery-dependent indices.

Growth information is supplied externally. Natural mortality is age-dependent
and time-invariant, derived from a life-history meta-analysis and a selected
maximum age of 16; the review noted sensitivity of biomass and F estimates to
M. Reproductive potential is modeled as numbers of eggs produced, using
maturity, batch fecundity and spawning events by age. The report calls this
quantity SSB, but it is not conventional biomass SSB in tonnes. Source
weight-at-age inputs and numeric annual N-at-age/F-at-age output arrays were
not verified during this screen.

The assessment and peer-review report include annual F and egg-production
trajectories, reference points and stock-status diagnostics. These values
could be useful if SEDAR 82 is confirmed as the applicable production run, but
egg production must remain clearly distinguished from tinyAM's biomass SSB.
