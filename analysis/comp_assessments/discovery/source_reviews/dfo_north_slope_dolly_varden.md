# DFO North Slope Dolly Varden — composite inventory entry

## Stock identity and assessment boundary

The DFO Sustainability Survey entry “North Slope Dolly Varden — Big Fish,
Rat, Firth, Babbage, Vittrekwa” is a management grouping, not one assessment
unit. DFO's 2019 Integrated Fisheries Management Plan identifies separate river
populations, including Big Fish, Rat, Firth, Babbage, Vittrekwa, and Fish Creek
(Komakuk). The plan lists distinct assessment histories: Rat River was last
formally assessed with data through 2014 (review held in 2015 and published as
Science Advisory Report 2016/058 in 2017); Big Fish River has a 2013 assessment;
Babbage River a 2017 assessment; and Firth River a 2003 assessment. No formal
assessment was listed for Vittrekwa, while Fish Creek studies began in 2016.
The IFMP is updated to 2019 and reports population information through 2016.

The inventory row is therefore marked identity-ambiguous and is not assigned a
single candidate-unit ID. The Rat River population is a separate, promising
follow-up candidate, but it is not silently substituted for the entire DFO row.
No current canonical Dolly Varden stock was found in the database.

## Rat River evidence

The 2017 advisory report covers the 2009–2014 assessment review. It describes
three population models, including a statistical catch-at-age model, and reports
population abundance, age-1 recruits, SSB, fishing status, and exploitation
outputs. Its 2025 erratum corrected several values and replaced the model-output
figure. A later DFO Research Document (2025/050) applies DB-SRA, a surplus
production model, and an integrated statistical catch-at-age model to Rat River
data through 2014. It documents:

- Total subsistence harvest estimates in numbers for 1986–2014.
- Standardized fishery-dependent CPUE in fish per set and catch-at-age
  proportions for the iSCAM over 1996–2014.
- Catch-age observations limited to 113 mm and 127 mm gillnets; age classes
  3–13 are described, with age 10 and older pooled as 10+.
- Age-specific maturity and weight/growth relationships, age-specific natural
  mortality assumptions, age-1 recruitment, total abundance, SSB, and fishing
  mortality/exploitation outputs.
- A monitoring timing of 0.62, based on median observation day August 14.

The harvest table and biological source documents are available as local PDFs.
However, the annual catch-age observations and several model outputs are
presented in figures; exact numeric matrices and run files have not been
verified. The 2025 analysis is a DFO Research Document, but its relationship to
the model accepted in the formal advice record needs confirmation before those
outputs are treated as the production advice series. The 2020 research documents provide the supporting catch-effort, biological, and mark-recapture
information used for the 2009–2014 period.

DFO published Research Document 2026/053 on Rat River mark-recapture and
biological information for 2015–2022. This is a newer detailed source for the
same current unit, so its inputs and outputs should be reviewed before any
fallback or selection decision. Its official catalogue page is cached locally.
The 15.20 MB PDF exceeded the available web retrieval limit, and direct local
requests to the publication endpoint redirected to the archived-information
page; the 2025/050 PDF endpoint did the same. The report body and whether it
contains any revised population assessment remain unverified.

## Screening decision

The composite inventory row is `ambiguous` and its overall eligibility remains
`unresolved`; it is not classified as age-structured-ineligible because the Rat
River component has a documented iSCAM. The Rat River is a potential separate
candidate for source curation, subject to establishing the correct unit-level
register entry, verifying all numerical inputs and official-output provenance,
and reviewing the 2026/053 report. Do not count this composite row as one
eligible new stock or add numerical canonical records at this stage.
