# NOAA Caribbean silk and queen snapper

Review date: 2026-10-31

## Unit and latest assessment

NOAA Stock SMART identifies Caribbean silk snapper (*Lutjanus vivanus*; stock
10241) and Caribbean queen snapper (*Etelis oculatus*; stock 10247) as separate
management units. SEDAR 26 is the latest completed detailed assessment located
for these units. The assessment was completed in 2011; NOAA InPort records the
project as completed and gives 2011 as its effective date. SEDAR 103 is a
successor process for alternate methods for Caribbean island-based species, but
as of this review its application webinars are scheduled from November 2026
through March 2027, followed by review in 2027. It has not yet produced
completed advice that supersedes SEDAR 26.

SEDAR 26 covers Puerto Rico, St. Croix, and St. Thomas/St. John. These island
platforms are distinct analysis areas within the Caribbean management units;
they are not separate NOAA Stock SMART rows. The review therefore retains one
candidate unit for each species and records the island-level limitations.

## Source screen

SEDAR 26 used data-limited approaches. In Puerto Rico, the quantitative
analyses focused on commercial CPUE indices and length-frequency methods. The
data workshop states that catch-at-age estimates were not developed for the
Puerto Rico commercial silk snapper catches; the report also describes limits
to species-specific recreational and U.S. Virgin Islands catch information.
For queen snapper, the quantitative analyses likewise used commercial CPUE
and length-frequency data. Species-specific U.S. Virgin Islands landings and
indices could not be developed from the group-reported landings.

The SEDAR 26 reviewer concluded that the available data were too limited for
standard fishery stock-assessment models. The report does not provide the
age-disaggregated catch and survey inputs, age-specific abundance and fishing
mortality outputs, or spawning-biomass series needed for an age-structured
tinyAM comparison. Life-history literature and length-based total-mortality
analyses do not supply those missing model surfaces.

Both units are screened ineligible for the age-disaggregated comparison set.
That decision reflects the completed assessment and publicly recoverable
inputs and outputs; it does not imply that no age or biological observations
exist in the broader research record.

## Decision

These are **new, ineligible discovery candidates**, not canonical database
records. Their assessment inputs and outputs were not imported. The 2026
SEDAR 103 work should be checked again when its application and review stages
are complete.
