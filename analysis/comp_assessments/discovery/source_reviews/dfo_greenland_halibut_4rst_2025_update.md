# DFO Greenland halibut, Gulf of St. Lawrence (4RST; latest update in 2025)

## Unit and assessment

This is a distinct Gulf of St. Lawrence stock unit in NAFO divisions 4RST. DFO
describes it as genetically distinct from the main Northwest Atlantic Greenland
halibut population. The canonical database has no 4RST Greenland halibut
record, so this is a provisional new unit. It is not the Bering Sea/Aleutian
Islands Greenland turbot assessed by NOAA.

The latest detailed report is the 2024 stock assessment, reviewed on
February 20–21, 2025 and published as Research Document 2026/033. A separate
interim update uses the 2025 survey and was published as Science Response
2026/013. The 2025/012 Science Advisory Report labels the 2024 meeting a full
assessment but lists February 2023 as the last full assessment. The 2026/013
Science Response calls the 2025 update interim and lists 2025 as the last full
assessment. These report fields are inconsistent; the register records the
latest detailed report and the later 2025 status update separately.

The assessment approach is index-based: empirical, fishery-independent survey
indices are used for stock-status advice. This is not an age-structured
population-dynamics model.

## Inputs and assumptions relevant to an age-indexed model

- **Catch:** The 2024 assessment reports commercial landings for 1970–2024;
  the 2025 update extends the landings series through 2025. The detailed report
  also presents commercial catch-at-length summaries. Neither report
  identifies catch numbers-at-age or an age-composition likelihood for the
  assessment.
- **Survey information:** The 2024 assessment uses the August ecosystemic
  survey in the Estuary and northern Gulf (nGSL) for 1990–2024; the 2025 update
  extends that status-indicator series through 2025. The detailed report also
  presents abundance and biomass indices by size class from the nGSL survey
  (1984–2024), the southern Gulf survey (1971–2024), and the Mobile Sentinel
  Program survey (1995–2024). The 0–20 cm and 20–30 cm bins are discussed as
  approximate one- and two-year-old fish, but they are size bins, not observed
  age-specific abundance indices. Age and maturity analyses in the detailed
  report are supporting biological analyses, not an age-composition likelihood
  for the assessment.
- **Weights and maturity:** The detailed report includes survey biomass by
  size class and analyses of maturity from biological samples. No run-specific
  weight-at-age or maturity-at-age input to an age-structured population model
  was identified. The 2024 advisory report states that more maturity, growth
  and age data would be needed to develop a population-dynamics model.
- **Mortality:** The index-based assessment does not estimate an
  age-structured natural-mortality process. Its relative exploitation
  indicator is calculated from landings and survey biomass and is not an
  instantaneous fishing-mortality estimate.
- **Timing:** The nGSL ecosystemic survey is conducted in August. The reports
  do not specify a fractional-year timing for a population model; timing is
  not part of this index-only assessment.

## Reported outputs

The 2025 interim update reports a stock-status indicator of 7,320 t for
biomass above 40 cm, with a plotted 95% confidence interval, and estimates a
99% probability that the indicator is below the 10,000 t limit reference
point. The detailed 2024 report gives 11,240 t for this indicator and a
relative exploitation rate of 0.06%. The 2024 advisory report also shows an
age-1 recruitment indicator from the nGSL survey, in millions of fish. These
are empirical stock-status indicators, not estimated population SSB,
recruitment, or N/F/M-at-age outputs. DFO notes that survey catchability is
unknown, so the biomass-above-40-cm indicator is only a relative approximation
of spawning biomass.

## Screening decision

This unit is **ineligible for the age-disaggregated candidate set**. Detailed
current sources exist and provide useful aggregate landings, survey indices,
size structure, a stock-status indicator, and an age-1 recruitment index.
However, the assessment does not use catch-at-age or an age-specific survey
likelihood, and it does not report age-specific population outputs. The
biological age and maturity analyses do not change that assessment structure.
No canonical numerical records were imported during this discovery screen.
