# Pacific ocean perch (U.S. West Coast): 2017 source screen

## Stock unit and latest assessment

NOAA Stock SMART identifies stock 10545 as Pacific ocean perch on the U.S.
Pacific Coast. NOAA Fisheries still cites the 2017 stock assessment as the
latest assessment for this unit; its current status page also reports status
based on 2023 catch. The 2017 STAR panel describes one U.S. West Coast unit and
states that it is assessed independently of the British Columbia and Alaska
populations. It is distinct from the existing DFO British Columbia and NOAA
Alaska Pacific ocean perch records.

## What the 2017 assessment provides

The final assessment used Stock Synthesis 3.30.03.05. It was a two-sex,
age-structured model beginning in 1918, with an accumulated age group at 60.
Natural mortality was fixed at 0.054 per year. The report identifies material
uncertainty in stock status related to steepness; the base model fixed
steepness at 0.72 and reports sensitivity runs.

| Evidence | Assessment detail |
|---|---|
| Catch and age data | Annual catch histories are reported in tonnes. The model also fits sex-specific age-composition data for the commercial fishery, at-sea hake fleet, and surveys. The report gives composition figures and sample summaries, but not a complete numeric catch-at-age matrix. |
| Survey information | The base model uses aggregate abundance indices from several surveys. Survey age data include marginal age compositions and conditional age-at-length data, including NWFSC shelf-slope observations from 2003–2016. Survey series and age samples must remain separate. |
| Weights and growth | The report gives sex-specific weight-at-length relationships and estimated length-at-age. Converting these to weight-at-age requires a documented transformation; a supplied annual weight-at-age input matrix was not found. |
| Maturity and fecundity | Maturity is modeled as a length-based relationship informed by 537 biological samples. Spawning output is in millions of eggs, using a length-based fecundity relationship; it is not SSB in tonnes. |
| Timing | Survey timing differs by series. The report describes the AFSC slope survey in October–November and the NWFSC slope survey in summer. A single observation timing for all indices would not be supported. |
| Outputs | The report provides annual spawning output, depletion, recruitment, and uncertainty summaries. Appendix D lists female and male numbers-at-age CSVs and the SS3 input/control files as auxiliary files, but those files were not located in the public links reviewed. |

## Screening decision

This is one new, distinct assessment unit and its 2017 report is the latest
assessment NOAA currently cites. The age-structured assessment and report
figures show a potential route to recover age-composition inputs alongside
annual catches, survey indices, and life-history relationships. However, the
numeric model input file and the auxiliary numbers-at-age outputs have not been
recovered. The candidate therefore remains eligibility-unresolved pending a
search for those files or verification that the report data can be digitized
with sufficient coverage. No canonical assessment, input, or output records
were added, and no tinyAM translation was attempted.
