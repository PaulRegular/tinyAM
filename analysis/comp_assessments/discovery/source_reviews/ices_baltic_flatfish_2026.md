# ICES Baltic Sea stock indicators and flatfish

Review date: 2026-10-30

## Current 2026 records screened

The 2026 ICES Standard Graphs list contains five current Baltic stock records in this group. Their pages report stock-level biomass or stock-size indicators, aggregate catches or landings, and—in some cases—length-based or relative fishing-pressure measures. The published F-at-age and F-at-length fields are blank. None of these pages provides age-specific observed inputs or N-at-age, F-at-age, or M-at-age surfaces. Detailed assessment input files were not recovered in this screen.

These five records are **ineligible for the current age-disaggregated assessment comparison** on the published evidence reviewed. This does not establish that biological age samples do not exist.

## Remaining Baltic inventory entries

The other five 2026 SID entries were checked against the latest detailed
working-group reports. The WGBFAS 2026 report says that no advice was requested
for Baltic dab or brill, but data were updated and update assessments were
conducted. Dab was assessed with SPiCT using aggregate catch and exploitable-
biomass information; the report describes annual length-weight relationships,
not age-specific population surfaces. Brill is assessed from trend information;
its BITS indicator counts fish at least 20 cm per hour and is age-independent.
Neither report chapter verifies catch-at-age, an age-specific abundance index,
age-specific biology, or N/F/M-at-age output for the current assessment.
Both units are screened ineligible for the current age-disaggregated
population-assessment comparison. This does not establish that biological age
samples do not exist.

For dab and brill, the WGBFAS 2026 report is the latest detailed report reviewed.
For salmon and sea trout, WGBAST 2025 is the latest detailed assessment report;
the 2026 report says there were no new assessments.

The WGBAST 2026 report states that the group's work was devoted to benchmark
tasks and that no new salmon or trout assessments were carried out. It points
to the 2025 annual report as the source of best available science. The 2025
report documents age-read catch samples for salmon, including scales used for
age reading, but the catch samples alone do not establish a representative,
annual catch-at-age likelihood series for either broad ICES unit. Main Basin
salmon (SD 22–31) is assessed through river life-history modelling for
assessment units 1–4 and expert elicitation for AU 5; Gulf of Finland salmon
(SD 32/AU 6) is assessed through expert elicitation. The published outputs are
river and life-history quantities and stock status, not complete conventional
N/F/M-at-age surfaces. These two salmon units remain screened with eligibility
unresolved until the age-sample coverage, assessment likelihood role, and
possibility of a comparable age-specific output are verified from the source
data and model material. This distinction matters: age samples exist, but that
alone does not establish a usable age-structured assessment input set.

For sea trout, the 2025 report presents catches, biological sampling,
recruitment-status monitoring and river-level status summaries; the 2026 report
contains no new assessment. No fitted age-structured population model,
age-specific survey abundance series, or comparable N/F/M-at-age output was
verified. The unit is screened ineligible for the current age-disaggregated
population-assessment comparison. This does not establish that biological age
samples do not exist.

The five Standard Graphs units and five additional SID units have no matching
canonical stock records. No canonical numerical records were added. The five
additional units are linked to stable candidate-unit IDs in `candidates.csv`;
the two salmon units have unresolved age-input eligibility, while sea trout,
dab, and brill are ineligible on the evidence reviewed.
