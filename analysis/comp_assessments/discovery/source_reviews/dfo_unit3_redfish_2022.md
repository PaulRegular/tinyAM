# DFO Unit 3 Redfish (status update for 2022)

## Identity and latest official reporting

The DFO 2024 Sustainability Survey lists Acadian Redfish - Unit 3 as a current Maritimes stock unit. The latest public stock-status update located is the 2022 update published as DFO Science Response 2023/022. DFO's 2026-2027 work plan schedules revision of the Unit 3 limit reference point, but this is not a new production assessment.

The management area is Unit 3, including NAFO Division 4X and DFO statistical unit areas 4Wdehkl. No matching Unit 3 stock record was found in the canonical stock table, so this is provisionally a new current management unit. The distinction between the name Acadian Redfish and the observed assessment unit needs care: the 2022 report states that Acadian and Deepwater redfish are not separated in commercial or research-survey catches.

## Age-disaggregated input screen

The 2022 status update uses total landings and the DFO summer RV survey. It reports survey total and mature biomass indices and abundance-at-length; the fishery section reports catch-at-size summaries. The survey ran in July and August. The report does not provide a fitted age-structured assessment, catch-at-age, an age-specific abundance index, or age-specific fitted population outputs.

The mature biomass index is based on survey abundance at sizes above 22 cm fork length and a combined-sex length-weight relationship derived from 1992-2019 data. This is a size-based maturity proxy and length-weight conversion, not maturity-at-age or weights-at-age. The report's relative fishing mortality metric is landings divided by a smoothed mature biomass index; it is not age-specific F. No natural-mortality process is fitted.

The 2015 framework proceedings document data review and exploratory modelling, but state that Unit 3 model development and a stock-status assessment were planned for 2016-2017. They do not establish run-matched age-specific model inputs or outputs for the current status update. The case is therefore ineligible for the current age-disaggregated tinyAM candidate set on the public sources reviewed. It remains a useful candidate for a future length-based analysis if detailed data become available.
