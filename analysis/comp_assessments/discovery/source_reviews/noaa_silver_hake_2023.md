# NOAA northern and southern silver hake

Reviewed 2026-10-29. NOAA assesses silver hake as two separate U.S. management stocks: the Gulf of Maine/northern Georges Bank stock and the southern Georges Bank/Mid-Atlantic stock. NOAA identifies them separately on its current [species page](https://www.fisheries.noaa.gov/species/silver-hake).

## Current assessment and data

The latest detailed assessment update located is the 2023 Management Track update, with commercial catch and NEFSC survey indices through 2022. The report says the northern and southern updates were not peer reviewed in fall 2023, but NOAA's current species page still cites the 2023 assessment for stock status. The updates continue the accepted empirical index-based approach from the 2010 benchmark, using three-year averages of catch and NEFSC fall survey biomass. They are not analytical age-structured assessments.

For northern silver hake, the 2023 report gives a 2020-2022 mean fall biomass index of 28.64 kg/tow and exploitation ratio of 0.11. For southern silver hake, the corresponding values are 1.036 kg/tow and 3.878. These are index-based status measures, not estimates of total population biomass or age-specific fishing mortality.

The 2017 update shows NEFSC autumn survey numbers-at-age for 1963-2015. These survey age data are supporting biological/index information, not fishery catch-at-age, and the current empirical assessment does not use them to estimate a population-at-age. The 2010 benchmark attempted an ASAP age-structured model, but it was not accepted because the age profiles in fishery and survey data could not be reconciled. The current update reports aggregate landings/discards/catch and survey biomass; no current fit-matched fishery catch-at-age, age-specific abundance likelihood, age-specific population output, weights-at-age, or maturity-at-age were verified.

The southern stock includes offshore hake in catch advice, which is an additional limitation for interpreting the aggregate southern catch. The assessment reports the index in kg/tow and catch in metric tons. Survey timing is autumn, but no fractional observation timing is specified.

Both units are screened ineligible for the current age-disaggregated assessment set. This decision is about the current production assessment and does not imply that NOAA has no supporting survey-age data. The DFO 4VWX Scotian Shelf unit is kept separate: similar species and geographic adjacency do not prove a shared assessment boundary.

No canonical database records or numerical series were imported.
