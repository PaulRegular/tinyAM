# NOAA Alaska shark candidates: BSAI and GOA

Review date: 2026-11-02

## Assessment run and stock units

The detailed assessment reviewed is the November 2022 AFSC SAFE chapter for
the Bering Sea/Aleutian Islands (BSAI) and Gulf of Alaska (GOA) shark stock
complexes. The 2024 BSAI SAFE introduction describes a catch/specification
update and says the 2022 full assessment was carried forward for 2025–2026.
The 2026 groundfish SAFE chapter list reviewed for this screen does not include
a shark chapter. Accordingly, the 2022 report remains the latest detailed full
assessment located as of this review; the older Stock SMART assessment-year
labels for the complex rows are not treated as run years.

The BSAI management complex includes Pacific sleeper shark, spiny dogfish,
salmon shark and other/unidentified sharks. Its Tier 6 harvest specifications
are catch-based. Pacific sleeper shark is also shown as a species component
with a catch-only model; the assessment states that age data are not available
for this species. The GOA complex differs: spiny dogfish is assessed as a Tier
5 component using a random-effects biomass model, while the other shark
components are Tier 6 catch-based. These nested species components and
multi-species complexes remain distinct candidate definitions in the register;
they are not four extra assessments of one age-structured stock.

## Age-disaggregated input and output screen

The 2022 report includes aggregate catches, survey biomass or relative
population numbers, length distributions and life-history summaries. The
length observations and biological age information do not form catch-at-age or
age-composition likelihood inputs. No age-specific abundance index is fitted.
The reported results are catch-based harvest limits or aggregate biomass and
status summaries, not annual N-at-age, F-at-age, recruitment or spawning
biomass surfaces. Therefore NOAA:10696, NOAA:11403, NOAA:10700 and NOAA:11450
are screened ineligible for the age-disaggregated candidate set.

The 2024 report documents the later catch/specification update and carry-forward
of previous assessment results. NOAA's shark research page describes the
managed complexes and the data limitations for Pacific sleeper shark. The
2026 SAFE chapter page was checked for a newer detailed shark chapter.
