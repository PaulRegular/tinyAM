# NOAA Cook Inlet EEZ salmon candidates (2026 SAFE)

## Official assessment and source set

This review covers the five NOAA Stock SMART entries for the Cook Inlet EEZ:
aggregate Chinook, Kenai River late-run sockeye, Kasilof River sockeye,
aggregate other sockeye, and aggregate coho. The latest detailed federal source
reviewed is the [final 2026 Cook Inlet Salmon SAFE](https://files.npfmc.org/SAFE/2026/CookInletSalmon.pdf),
which assesses the 2025 fishery and provides 2026 harvest specifications. The
assessment inventory snapshot lists 2026 runs through data year 2024; this
one-year metadata difference is retained in the candidate register, and the
final SAFE is used to describe the actual current report.

Supporting sources are the [ADF&G 2023 Upper Cook Inlet escapement-goal
review (FMS 24-01)](https://www.adfg.alaska.gov/FedAidPDFs/FMS24-01.pdf) and
the [ADF&G 2023 commercial fisheries annual report (FMR 25-05)](https://www.adfg.alaska.gov/FedAidPDFs/FMR25-05.pdf).
The PDFs, retrieval dates, URLs, and SHA-256 checksums are in the ignored
`source_cache/noaa_cook_inlet_salmon_2026/` directory.

## Evidence and limits

The final SAFE is not a single age-structured population assessment for these
five units. Kenai and Kasilof sockeye are Tier 1 stocks: the federal SAFE uses
AR-1 forecasts of total run size and a beta-distributed state harvest rate to
set harvest specifications. It describes age-composition data and brood tables
for both stocks. FMS 24-01 reports brood-year totals for escapement and returns,
not an annual N-at-age model surface. FMR 25-05 Appendix A13 reports sockeye
passage age composition for 2023 (ages 3–6, with freshwater/ocean-age
subclasses); its Appendix A14 reports average weights by area, not by age. A
complete year-by-year age-specific catch or abundance series, maturity-at-age,
and age-specific M were not recovered from this reviewed source set. These two
stock-specific records remain unresolved pending recovery of the underlying
annual age datasets and confirmation of compatible biological inputs.

The aggregate Chinook assessment is a Tier 3 stock-complex assessment based on
EEZ harvest and indicator-stock escapement. The SAFE notes that the EEZ catch
cannot be reliably attributed to component stocks. Stock SMART labels its 2026
entry “Cohort Analysis,” which does not match the final SAFE's described Tier 3
method; the exact inventory-run mapping is unresolved.

Aggregate other sockeye is assessed with Tier 3 harvest and indicator
escapement. The SAFE confirms that age and genetic samples exist for commercial
harvest and that age estimates exist for some tributaries, but it does not
provide an age-specific abundance series for the aggregate complex. Aggregate
coho is also assessed with Tier 3 indicators; recent indicator escapement data
are incomplete, and the 2026 SAFE does not estimate a total run size. For both
complexes, no unit-wide age-specific input series, mortality surface, or
age-based population output was recovered. They are ineligible for the
age-structured candidate set on the available official evidence.

All five current SAFEs report useful official aggregate outputs such as
harvest, escapement indicators, total-run forecasts where applicable, and
federal harvest specifications. None reports an annual age-specific N, F, or M
surface. No numerical assessment inputs or outputs were added to the canonical
database during this inventory screen.

Eligibility here means readiness for the age-structured candidate set, not
whether an official aggregate assessment or useful source data exist. Revisit
the two sockeye records if complete annual age-specific data and compatible
biological inputs are found.
