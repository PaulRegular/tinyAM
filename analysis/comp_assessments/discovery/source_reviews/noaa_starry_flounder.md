# Starry flounder: current assessment units and age-data screen

## Identity and current assessment

The NOAA Stock SMART snapshot contains three entries for *Platichthys stellatus*. ID 10025 is the broad Pacific Coast 2005 benchmark entry. IDs 14804 and 14805 are the 2017 Northern Pacific Coast and Southern Pacific Coast DB-SRA assessment entries. The 2017 report treats Oregon-Washington and California as separate regional models, then combines their MSY contributions for coastwide harvest specifications. The old broad entry is therefore not a third current unit; it is retained as an ambiguous historical identity linked to the two current entries.

The 2025 Groundfish SAFE describes the 2017 DB-SRA as the assessment used for management since 2019. The Council's March 2026 analysis for the 2027-28 biennium still cites the 2017 DB-SRA for starry flounder. This is the latest detailed assessment used for current advice located in this review. The 2025 SAFE also reports that the previous 2005 assessment used catch, relative trawl-logbook abundance indices, and a California age-1 abundance index, but no age- or length-composition data. The current DB-SRA is not the 2005 age-structured model.

## Inputs and biological assumptions

The 2017 DB-SRA is based on historical aggregate removals in metric tonnes and life-history distributions. Its report reconstructs commercial and recreational catches, including assumed discard rates and imputed historical values. The Northern (Oregon-Washington) series extends to 1889, with constant equilibrium catch assumed before 1970 in the model; the California series extends to 1900, with an equilibrium-catch assumption before 1947. These are aggregate catch histories, not numbers-at-age.

The DB-SRA requires age at maturity and distributions for natural mortality, FMSY/M, BMSY/B0, and depletion. Age at maturity is set to 3 years. It does not use annual weights-at-age, maturity-at-age, a survey index, age compositions, or catch-at-age. Although the earlier 2005 model used an age-1 index in California, that index is not part of the current DB-SRA and does not provide a current age-specific survey series.

## Outputs and eligibility

The 2017 report gives median MSY of 298 mt (95% highest-density interval 237–359 mt) for Oregon-Washington and 353 mt (295–526 mt) for California. The 2026 harvest-specification analysis continues to cite the 2017 assessment and reports coastwide 2027 and 2028 OFL of 652 mt, ABC of 393 mt and ACL of 393 mt. These are management and yield outputs, not annual abundance-at-age, fishing-mortality-at-age, or natural-mortality-at-age series.

The current Northern and Southern units are ineligible for the planned age-structured comparison because the latest assessment used for advice is catch-based and does not expose age-disaggregated inputs or outputs. The canonical assessment database is not changed by this screen. NOAA ID 10025 is marked ambiguous and ineligible as a current candidate so it is not counted as a third stock.
