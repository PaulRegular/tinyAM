## Official assessment and source trail

The 2026 WGBFAS report is the latest detailed assessment recovered for the
accepted advice. The ICES Stock Annex identifies the SD32 survey configuration
as the reference run and says the assessment used for advice is an ensemble of
three SS3 models. Those models share their data and configuration and differ in
their age- and year-varying natural-mortality surface. The 2023 WKBBALTPEL
benchmark established the SS3 framework.

The report explains that annual total catches have stated standard errors of
0.2 for initial catches, 0.1 for 1903–1973 and 0.05 from 1974 onward; its text
does not establish the scale of those values. The initial catches use the
1903–1905 average. Age-composition sample sizes are the number of trips for
commercial catches and hauls for the survey, but the annual numerical values
were not recovered. A Dirichlet-multinomial distribution weights age
compositions. BIAS index CVs were estimated by bootstrapping; the annual CV
series was not recovered.

The model is age-based, single-sex (combined), single-area, single-commercial-
fleet and single-survey. Recruitment is age 0, generated by a Beverton–Holt
stock-recruitment relationship with estimated steepness and autocorrelation;
process SD is fixed at 0.5. Recruitment deviations are estimated from 1974 to
the current year and for 1968–1983. The initial population is assumed exploited.
F uses an age-specific selection pattern and reports a simple, unweighted
Fbar over ages 3–6. The report describes time-invariant fishery selectivity and
random-walk log-selectivity over age for fishery and survey, with logistic
selection at ages 5–8+.

## Recovered official outputs

WGBFAS Tables 4.2.11a–b provide age-0 recruitment, SSB, Fbar ages 3–6, relative
SSB to MSY Btrigger, relative F to FMSY, and total catch. Recruitment and SSB
include the reported 5th, median and 95th percentiles. These are central 90%
ensemble intervals, not 95% confidence intervals or standard errors. The
report marks 2025–2026 recruitment as predicted from the stock-recruitment
relationship. The 2026 row has forecast recruitment and SSB but no Fbar or
catch. The relative SSB values refer to 1 January; absolute SSB is provided for
ecosystem analyses and is not the basis for advice.

No age-specific N, F or M output surfaces were found in the report or ICES
Standard Graph source data. The numerical outputs stored in `outputs.csv`
therefore remain partial relative to the full assessment.

## Validation and remaining gaps

The importer validates the reported year coverage, age dimensions, three-M
scenario ordering, repeated M boundary years, known table values, and 2025
summary values. It also checks that the row-wise age values remain aligned with
their year-age keys when the published matrices are expanded. The stock-specific
source test checks units, source labels, coverage, duplicated keys, interval
meaning, and missingness.

The database status is `partial` for assumptions, inputs and outputs. Missing
items include the 1903 total catch, the numerical age-composition sample sizes
and weights, annual acoustic CVs and likelihood weights, native SS3 files, and
age-specific N/F/M outputs. The source PDF and ICES Standard Graph page are
available; no separate machine-readable SS3 data or model object was recovered.

The main-report text conflicts with its maturity table and with the Stock
Annex's early weight extension. Numerical tabled inputs were retained where
available; for historical weight values the Stock Annex's 1974 extension was
used and the report-text difference is documented above. These choices should
be revisited if the native SS3 input files become public.
## tinyAM translation and fit

The translation fits 1974–2025 and ages 0–8+, preserving the source catch-at-age, BIAS index, weights, maturity, and the medium fixed-M scenario. It omits zero age-0 acoustic values from the lognormal observation table. BIAS timing is set to 0.8 as an explicit approximation for October. The model uses an exponential initial age structure, no separate N survival process, Beverton–Holt recruitment with AR1 residuals and fixed process SD 0.5, an AR1 F process with a logistic age mean, and age-specific BIAS q. Catch and index observation SDs are estimated as common groups. No source-based starting values were used.

The default fit converged: optimizer code 0, objective 157.39, maximum absolute gradient 1.18e-6, positive-definite Hessian, and successful standard-error estimation. `check_tam()` also reports a stock-recruit parameter interval wider than tenfold, an AR1 correlation interval wider than 0.5, some residual groups with standardized spread outside 0.5–1.5, and some standardized residuals above 3 in absolute value. The fit is numerically stable, while recruitment shape and persistence remain weakly identified.

Recruitment uses age 0 in the same calendar year, but its 2025 official value is a stock-recruit prediction. The M surface matches because it is supplied from the same medium-M scenario; it is not evidence that tinyAM independently estimated M. The official Fbar is an unweighted age-3–6 mean, while tinyAM reports an abundance-weighted mean, and no source F-at-age or N-at-age surface was recovered. The comparison does not establish likelihood equivalence.
