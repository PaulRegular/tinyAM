# Common sole in ICES Subdivisions 20–24

## Assessment selected

The stock is Common sole (*Solea solea*), ICES `sol.27.20-24`, in Subdivisions
20–24. WGBFAS 2025 is the latest detailed official assessment recovered. Its
accepted SAM run uses observations through 2024 and reports fitted summaries
for 1984–2024. The cached fit converged (optimizer code 0; objective 45.71169)
and reproduces the report's annual recruitment, SSB and Fbar table within its
published rounding. The native N-at-age values also agree with the report.

WGBFAS 2026 confirms an accepted SAM update for this stock (report pp. 663–664).
The 2026 ICES Stock Assessment Graphs series provides aggregate recruitment,
SSB and Fbar through 2025 and a 2026 summary projection, but no detailed native
2026 input or age-specific output files were recovered. Those values are stored
under a separate summary-only assessment and are not mixed with the 2025 fit.
The WGBFAS 2026 audit also notes that a new survey index and revised reference
points introduced in 2025 make SSB and fishing-pressure estimates for 2025
incomparable with earlier assessment estimates.

## Recovered inputs and assumptions

- Residual catch is landings only, in thousand fish, ages 2–9+ for 1984–2024;
  discards are excluded from the fitted assessment. The source observation
  table contains 328 finite catch-at-age values.
- The Q4 Sole survey index covers ages 1–9+ in 2004–2024. Eighteen cells from
  2012–2013 are missing and remain blank. Its within-year timing is 0.8754645.
  The original age-year CVs and the SAM relative precision weights are both
  retained. The assessment normalizes CVs by their overall mean and uses
  `1/(CV/mean(CV))^2`; these weights scale relative precision rather than
  fixing the observation variance.
- Gillnet CPUE covers ages 2–8 in 1994–2007 at timing 0.56. Trawl CPUE covers
  ages 2–6 in 1987–2008 at timing 0.875. Both remain on their native relative
  index scales. The source has 725 catch and index observation rows, including
  the missing Q4 cells.
- Annual stock and catch weights cover ages 1–9+ and 1984–2024, in kg per fish.
  SAM estimates weight processes from these observations; the tinyAM
  translation uses the source weight values as fixed inputs. Natural mortality
  is fixed at 0.1 per year. Maturity is zero at ages 1–2 and one at ages 3–9.
  The source supplies zero fractions of F and M before spawning.
- Recruitment follows the source random-walk model. N-process variance is
  separate at age 1 and shared across ages 2–9. F follows a random walk with
  compound-symmetry correlation across ages; F at age 1 is not estimated,
  ages 2–5 have separate states, and ages 6–9 share a state. The source
  observation residual structures are independent for catch and the Q4/trawl
  indices, with AR(1) residual correlation across ages for gillnet.

## Recovered outputs and completeness

The 2025 record contains fitted N-at-age and F-at-age for 1984–2024. Age 1 F is
not estimated in SAM and is left unavailable. Total abundance is derived by
summing native N-at-age; no uncertainty is available for this derived sum.
Table 6.10 supplies recruitment, SSB, Fbar for ages 4–8, and total biomass,
with published lower and upper 95% confidence limits. Fitted observation
predictions are retained for catch and all three indices. The age-state SEs in
the native object are on the log scale and are identified as such in the
output notes. M is fixed, so there is no estimated M output.

The 2025 assessment has complete recovered inputs and assumptions, and partial
outputs because its 2025 forecast tables are not included in the canonical
output series. The 2026 summary-only record has no recovered detailed inputs
or assumptions and partial aggregate outputs.

## tinyAM translation and fit

The translation fits 1984–2024 at ages 1–9+, preserves the source survey names,
timings, q-sharing groups, weights, maturity and fixed M, and omits only the 18
blank Q4 index cells from the likelihood. It uses IID cohort-survival
deviations, random-walk recruitment, fixed M, and separate observation SDs by
index. The closest correlated random-walk F process and an independent F
random-walk check both failed from package-default starts with an NA/NaN
gradient. No source starts were supplied. The final, documented approximation
uses a stationary AR1 F process over years and ages with one mean level; this is
less like the source's accumulated random walk, but it converges without custom
initialization.

The final fit converged with optimizer code 0, objective 485.4934, maximum
absolute gradient 0.000805, a positive-definite Hessian, successful SD
estimation, 23 fixed effects and 729 random effects. Residual checks flag some
age/series residual spreads outside 0.5–1.5, residuals above 3 SD, and strong
adjacent-year residual correlations in some groups.

Over 1984–2024, the common-definition comparisons are: SSB 7.5% mean absolute
percent difference, +11.2% terminal difference and 0.978 trend correlation;
recruitment 17.0%, +17.1% and 0.919; total abundance 9.8%, +11.3% and 0.967;
N-at-age 15.0%, +10.1% and 0.966; F-at-age 16.7%, −13.3% and 0.902; Fbar for
ages 4–8 8.5%, −11.8% and 0.948; and total biomass 8.4%, +10.7% and 0.970.
SSB and total biomass are approximate because tinyAM treats the reported stock
weights as fixed, while SAM estimates a weight process. Fbar is approximate
because the source report does not clarify whether its averaging weights
match tinyAM's abundance-weighted calculation. M and Mbar are fixed-input
consistency checks, not estimated-M comparisons. Total abundance is derived
from official N-at-age and has no source uncertainty interval.
